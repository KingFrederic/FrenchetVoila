# 🦊 Colors with Frederic — a 5-minute mini-lesson

**Subject:** Four French color words · **Learner:** one 5-year-old, no French background
**Duration:** ~5 minutes · **Guide:** *Frederic*
**Goal:** by the end, the child can hear a color word in French and point to it, and
can say *Rouge, Bleu, Jaune, Vert* out loud with a smile.

The child has no French, so **all the directions are in English** — the only French
in the whole lesson is the four color words themselves. That keeps the child confident
and clear about what to do, while the new French words ride along on top. Teach it
warmly and slowly, with lots of "Great job!" — at five, **confidence is the lesson**.

This is the script mapped onto the website so a parent, tutor, or classroom can run
it live.

---

## The four colors

| French word | Say it like | English | Picture anchor |
|-------------|-------------|---------|----------------|
| **Rouge** | *roozh* | Red | 🍓 a strawberry |
| **Bleu** | *bluh* | Blue | 🐳 a whale |
| **Jaune** | *zhohn* | Yellow | ☀️ the sun |
| **Vert** | *vair* | Green | 🐸 a frog |

> Why these four? They're the highest-contrast starter set (no blue/green or
> red/orange confusion), they map onto things a five-year-old already loves, and
> four is the sweet spot — enough to feel like an achievement, few enough to
> actually remember.

---

## Minute-by-minute

### ⏱ 0:00–0:45 — Hello! (warm-up)
- Open the site. Frederic waves: **"Hello!"** Wave back together.
- *"Today we're going to learn colors — in French! Ready? Let's go!"*
- Press **Start!** Keep it silly and low-stakes.

### ⏱ 0:45–2:30 — The Colors (flash cards)
The cards start showing only the **French color word**. This is the magic move:
> *"This card says* **Rouge**. *What color do you think is hiding? … Let's check!"*
- Child taps the card. It **flips to reveal the color**, a picture, and Frederic
  says the word in French. Celebrate the guess whether right or not.
- Repeat for all four. Each reveal, **say it together** twice: *"Rouge… rouge!"*
- Tie each color to its picture: *"Rouge — like a strawberry!"*
- When all four are flipped, Frederic cheers **"Great! You know all 4 colors!"**

**Teacher tip:** cover the word with your thumb and ask *"What color?"* to turn the
reveal into a guessing game. That single interaction is the whole method:
predict → reveal → confirm.

### ⏱ 2:30–4:30 — The Game (listen & find)
- Press **Let's play!** Four color squares appear, no words.
- Frederic gives the direction in English and names the color in French:
  **"Find… Rouge!"**
- Child taps the matching square. Correct → confetti + **"Yes! That's Rouge!"**;
  a miss just shakes gently and says **"Try again!"** — no losing, ever.
- Four rounds, one per color. Stars fill up as they go.

### ⏱ 4:30–5:00 — Great job! (close)
- Celebration screen with the score and confetti.
- Wrap up: *"You learned four colors in French today — Rouge, Bleu, Jaune, Vert!
  Give me a high five!"*
- Optional: **Play again**, or **Home** to start over.

## Teaching moves that make it land

- **Say it, don't spell it.** Five-year-olds learn sound first. Every color word is
  spoken by Frederic in French; echo it together, exaggerate the mouth shape.
- **Anchor to a picture.** "Bleu, like the whale" sticks far better than a rule.
- **English for the how, French for the what.** The child always knows what to *do*
  (English directions); the only new thing to *learn* is the color word.
- **No wrong answers.** Wrong taps are gentle nudges, never buzzers. Praise the
  attempt: *"Almost! Try the yellow one."*
- **Keep it to 5 minutes.** Stop while it's still fun. Come back tomorrow.

## Stretch it further (if they're hungry for more)
- Point at their clothes: *"What color is this? … Bleu!"*
- Count the colors in English while touching each card: one, two, three, four.
- Sing it: "Rouge, bleu, jaune, vert" to any tune they like.

## Assessment (how you know it worked)
- ✅ Can point to the right square when Frederic names a color (the game checks this).
- ✅ Can say at least two of the four color words unprompted.
- ✅ Wants to play again. At five, that's the real test.
